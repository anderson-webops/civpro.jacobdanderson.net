Instructor Guide

This guide is for a first classroom pilot, discussion section, office-hour exercise, or formative review. The goal is to make student choices visible and debatable, then use the round evidence to tune the game and the lesson.

Doctrine boundary: Trial Ready is a simplified learning game, not a legal expert system. It emphasizes issue recognition, procedural sequencing, factual application, and record building. Confirm course-specific nuance outside the game, especially in the Erie and class-action preview track.

Before class

Begin with the 30-minute Jurisdiction and Removal pack in Study mode. Its one round uses 5 minutes for briefing, 18 for play, and 7 for debrief. These are planning targets; record actual time. Repeat the pack to exchange roles. Longer packs rotate roles across their planned rounds.

  1. Open the game in the classroom browser and choose a curriculum track and class length.
  2. Keep the recommended replay seed unless you intentionally want a different deal. Record any custom seed in your lesson notes.
  3. Select Apply pack and start. Confirm that the active pack title, schedule, objectives, budgets, and planned round count appear.
  4. Choose whether to use timed play, Study mode, Exam mode, or hidden explanations. For a first pilot, Study mode is safest during the briefing and normal timed mode is useful for the second round.
  5. Select Print cards if teams will use physical cards. Printing uses only the active pack's case, attack, motion, and discovery subsets and includes the lesson seed and objectives.
  6. Print or share the student quickstart.
  7. Divide students into plaintiff and defense teams. Assign one student as docket reader and one as rule explainer if groups are larger than four.

How to facilitate a round

  1. Claim and defendant: Ask the plaintiff to identify the forum, citizenship facts, and why the selected defendant belongs in the case.
  2. Threshold window: Before the defense clicks, require a rule, supporting fact, and predicted procedural effect.
  3. Response: Ask whether the plaintiff's card defeats the attack, cures a defect, or merely redirects the case.
  4. Discovery: Require students to identify the proof item before selecting a tool. When an objection appears, separate compulsion, narrowing, and privilege-log choices.
  5. Rule 56: In at least one pilot round, close discovery before the checklist is complete. This makes the record consequence visible.
  6. Assessment: Stop after the outcome. Read doctrines triggered, wrong motions, missing proof, and governing authority IDs before rotating roles.

Assessment evidence

The post-round panel is formative evidence, not a grade by itself. It reports:

  • Doctrines triggered: Procedural issues actually activated by cards and outcomes.
  • Wrong motions or tools: Nonresponsive threshold motions and mismatched discovery choices.
  • Outcome: Dismissed or trial ready, with the controlling reason.
  • Missing proof: Checklist items absent when the round ended.
  • Governing sources: Rule or statute references tied to the played sequence.
  • Budget failures: Attempts that could not proceed because the role lacked game budget.

A useful debrief asks students to correct one wrong move, defend one correct move with a fact, and identify one result that reflects game abstraction rather than full doctrine.

Replay a confusing round

  1. At the confusing point or immediately after the outcome, select Export replay.
  2. Keep the JSON file with your pilot notes. The filename identifies the pack, seed, and round.
  3. Use Import replay to restore the exact phase, hands, budgets, case mutations, docket, and assessment.
  4. Read the structured event log in the replay file if you need the order of actions. Re-run the pack with the same seed to repeat the deal from the beginning.
  5. If the result remains surprising, record whether the problem is doctrine logic, unclear card language, balance, or facilitation.

Local-only playtest data

Completed-round statistics are stored only in the current browser. They are not submitted to a server, included in source imports, or published with the site. The panel keeps at most 200 rounds and can be cleared at any time.

Balance history contains counts and fixed game labels, not written reflections, custom seeds, or timestamps. Save/autosave and replay still contain written answers. Use a dedicated browser profile, omit personal information, keep exported files private, and use Clear local game data at the end of a shared-device session. Delete downloaded replays separately.

Clear stats before a comparable run; the panel combines stored rounds. Keep private observation notes on the pack, seed, mode, actual minutes, input method, and anonymous role counts. Publish only reviewed aggregate results and paraphrased issues. Do not commit completed observation sheets or learner replays to the public repository.

Suggested pilot feedback

After class, record these five items privately. Prefer reproducing issues with a synthetic replay instead of sharing learner writing:

  1. Pack, duration, seed, section size, and actual minutes used.
  2. One procedural choice students explained well.
  3. One card, instruction, or ruling students misunderstood.
  4. Whether the post-round assessment matched the discussion you wanted.
  5. One concrete change for the next pilot: wording, budget, timer, case, card subset, or facilitation prompt.

Contingencies

  • If the network is unavailable after the page loads, the game, save/load, stats, and printing continue locally. External source links will not open.
  • If time is short, close discovery early and use Rule 56 as the final decision point.
  • If students are stuck, enable explanations or Study mode rather than supplying the correct card immediately.
  • If a timer expires during discussion, load the saved state or import a replay and continue with timers paused.
  • If a replay import is rejected, confirm it is an unmodified Version 0.4 replay under 2 MB with the current content version.
